## Root cause: the onset function read link 1's frame-to-frame flicker as twelve onsets a second

Captured headless on the shipped build with the new `tapfade.mjs --dump` (every onset batch the worklet posted, with
both clocks as the detector read them) and `--pcm` (what Kari heard, through a ScriptProcessor on the controller's own
source node). `tools/probe/replay.mjs` feeds a dump back through the real `AutoBeatDetector` in Node and reproduces the
page's 18 runs on link 1 and 4 on link 2 to the digit, so everything below was read offline on exact copies of what the
page computed.

```
                                      link 1 (V3bLpIXaFp8)              link 2 (IcxJHQ0N13s)
raw ODF peaks over 1.0, first 30 s     375  (12.5 / s)                    120  (4.0 / s)
inter-onset intervals, top bins        30 ms ×93, 20 ms ×38, 40 ms ×35    110 ms ×20, 220 ms ×11  (16ths / 8ths at 136)
frames with any onset strength         73–81 of every 100                  51–85 (intro), then 57–62
novelty, 6 s window at 12 s            half of it in 49 frames; 104 > 0.3  half in 20 frames; 38 > 0.3
autocorrelation peaks (prior-free)     eight peaks, all r 0.21–0.27        89.6 r 0.45 · 136 r 0.39 · 67 r 0.43
best comb, any bpm                     P 0.82 · E 0.13 (123 bpm)          P 0.81 · E 0.27 (135 bpm)
```

The onset strength is the half-wave-rectified difference of each band's log energy against the *previous frame*. On
link 1 every band's energy wobbles up and down every one to three frames (a dense, sustained mix: the 20–40 ms
inter-onset bins are the band coming back up, not instruments), and each return counts as a rise, so three quarters of
all frames carry "onsets". The tracker's novelty is scaled so its 95th percentile is 1, which puts that floor at the
height of the real beats; a comb with ±1-frame teeth at a 49-frame period can touch at most 12 of ~100 active frames,
so E (the share of novelty the teeth explain) is capped near 0.13 whatever the period, the autocorrelation is flat
(no candidate is more periodic than the floor), and confidence = √(P·E)·√(r/0.5) sits at 0.17–0.27 under the 0.35 bar
for every window. Link 2 has a clean 16th-note grid (4 peaks a second), so the same code explains 27 % of its novelty
and commits.

What did **not** help (all run offline on the captures and on the test fixtures, `tools/probe` lab scripts in the
shared notes folder): longer windows (8 / 10 / 12 / 16 s: r stays 0.22–0.25, nothing stands out), a shorter moving
mean, a wider tooth, smoothing the novelty (kills the click-track fixtures), subtracting a percentile floor (link 2
flips to 67 bpm), squaring the novelty (noise confidence rises to 0.29 and link 2 flips to 67). The fault is upstream
of the tracker.

## The change: one constant in `OnsetFeature`

`src/content/beat/detect.ts`: `FLUX_MEMORY = 3`. Each band's rise is measured against the loudest of its last three
hops (30 ms) instead of the previous hop alone. A band that flickers up-down-up posts nothing on the way back up; a
real onset — a rise after a trough longer than 30 ms — is unchanged. The `prev` array becomes a 4×3 ring, `reset()`
clears it, nothing else moves. Raw peaks over 1.0 on link 1 drop from 364 to 66 in 30 s; on link 2 from 104 to 50.

Why 3 and not more: on the recorded audio every memory from 2 to 6 frames, and a 20–40 ms energy smoothing, commits
link 1 at 122 bpm at 16 s; but a memory of 6, or 40 ms of smoothing, tips link 2 from 135 to its 3:2 alias 90, and a
memory of 4 or more starts committing 61 bpm (the octave) through link 1's quiet passage at 26–30 s. Three frames is the
smallest value that commits link 1 and leaves link 2 and the fixtures where they were, with noise confidence lower.

The new test (`tests/detect.test.mjs`): pink noise amplitude-modulated at 50 Hz — energy up and down every hop, the
flicker in its purest form — must read at under 2× the plain noise's onset strength and never a confident tempo. On
the shipped detector it reads at 5.5× (2.92 vs 0.53) and the test fails; on the change it reads at 1.65× (0.33 vs 0.20)
and passes. The three click tracks, the swung

## Caveats, in order of weight

1. **122 is the detector's answer, not a verified one.** Jesse's count decides. 82 / 153 / 162 were aliases of it in
   the old runs; the first run on the fixed build still estimated 161.9 at confidence 0.3498, a hair under the bar —
   had it rounded the other way Kari would have shown 162 for ten seconds before refining. If his count is 122, that
   near-miss is the next thing to look at (the 4:3 alias of a dense track at the first run).
2. Link 2 now commits four seconds earlier at 136.3 and refines toward 135; the morning doc's 135 came from a run two
   seconds later. Which is right to the decimal needs his ear too.
3. The recording path is validated by correlation of the offline onset frames against the page's capture: 1.00 on the
   metronome, 0.93 on link 2, 0.61 on link 1 (a flickering ODF under a sub-frame alignment offset; peaks are one or two
   frames wide). Good enough to carry the tuning; the live stage runs above are the proof that matters.
4. Everything after link 1's commit (refinement through the 26–30 s quiet passage, the break at 32–34 s) is lab
   evidence only, because the stage probe stops at the first bpm.
